Sources

Every dismissal and every application, with where it comes from.

Quotes are graded by confidence in the Ledger. This page collects the underlying references. Where a popular version of a quote is apocryphal or an exaggeration, that's noted here too, not smoothed over.

Number theory → RSA

  • G. H. Hardy, A Mathematician's Apology, Cambridge University Press, 1940, §21, §25, §28 (and a footnote to §21 quoting his 1915 British Association address). Verified against Alan J. Cain (ed.), An Annotated Mathematician's Apology, 2019.
  • R. Rivest, A. Shamir, L. Adleman, “A Method for Obtaining Digital Signatures and Public-Key Cryptosystems,” Communications of the ACM 21(2), 1978, pp. 120–126. doi:10.1145/359340.359342.
  • Flagged as apocryphal: the line that Gauss called number theory the “queen of mathematics” for its supreme uselessness — Hardy himself, in the Apology, says he could never source an exact quotation for it.

Elliptic curves → ECC

  • N. Koblitz, A. Menezes, S. Vanstone, “Elliptic Curve Cryptography: The Serpentine Course of a Paradigm Shift,” IACR ePrint 2008/390.
  • A. Jurisic, A. Menezes, “Elliptic Curves and Cryptography,” Dr. Dobb's Journal, April 1997, pp. 23–36.
  • N. Koblitz, “Elliptic curve cryptosystems,” Mathematics of Computation 48(177), 1987, pp. 203–209. V. Miller, “Use of Elliptic Curves in Cryptography,” CRYPTO '85 Proceedings, Springer LNCS 218, 1986, pp. 417–426.
  • H. W. Lenstra, “Factoring integers with elliptic curves,” Annals of Mathematics 126, 1987, pp. 649–673.

Galois theory / finite fields → Reed–Solomon codes

  • Siméon Poisson's 1831 report to the Paris Academy of Sciences, rejecting Galois's memoir, as reproduced in MacTutor History of Mathematics, “Évariste Galois.”
  • I. S. Reed, G. Solomon, “Polynomial Codes over Certain Finite Fields,” Journal of the Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics 8(2), 1960, pp. 300–304.
  • NASA Technical Memorandum 102162, “Tutorial on Reed-Solomon Error Correction Coding,” 1990 (Voyager telemetry).
  • L. B. Vries, K. Odaka, “CIRC — the error-correcting code for the compact disc digital audio system,” AES Conference, June 1982.
  • QR code (Denso Wave, 1994) — Reed–Solomon-coded, tolerant of ~30% physical damage.

Group representation theory → crystallography & the Standard Model

  • W. Burnside, Theory of Groups of Finite Order, 1st ed., Cambridge University Press, 1897, Preface.
  • J. Slater, Solid-State and Molecular Theory: A Scientific Autobiography, Wiley, 1975 (the “Gruppenpest” anecdote).
  • E. Noether, “Invariante Variationsprobleme,” presented Göttingen, 16 July 1918.
  • E. S. Fedorov (1891) and A. Schoenflies (1891), independent classification of the 230 crystallographic space groups; see Space group.

Quaternions → 3D graphics, aerospace & robotics

  • Silvanus P. Thompson, The Life of William Thomson, Baron Kelvin of Largs, Macmillan, 1910 (source of the “unmixed evil” letter to Robert Baldwin Hayward, 1892).
  • K. Shoemake, “Animating Rotation with Quaternion Curves,” Computer Graphics (SIGGRAPH '85) 19(3), pp. 245–254.
  • Michael Collins on Apollo 11's gimbal-lock near-miss; see Gimbal lock.

Non-Euclidean / Riemannian geometry → General Relativity & GPS

  • Gauss to Bessel, 1829 (the “clamor of the Boeotians” letter); widely quoted in histories of non-Euclidean geometry, e.g. MacTutor's entry on the subject.
  • B. Riemann, “Über die Hypothesen, welche der Geometrie zu Grunde liegen,” lecture, 1854.
  • N. Ashby, “Relativity in the Global Positioning System,” Living Reviews in Relativity 6, 1, 2003.
  • Ohio State University, “Real-World Relativity: The GPS Navigation System” (NTS-2 satellite relativistic-correction experiment, 1977).

Knot theory → DNA topology & topological quantum computing

  • W. Thomson (Lord Kelvin), “On Vortex Atoms,” Proceedings of the Royal Society of Edinburgh, 1867.
  • P. G. Tait, knot tabulation, from ~1877 onward.
  • D. W. Sumners, DNA-topology work from the late 1980s–1990s (see Topoisomerase).
  • A. Kitaev, “Fault-tolerant quantum computation by anyons,” 1997, arXiv:quant-ph/9707021.

Topology → Topological Data Analysis

  • H. Poincaré, Analysis Situs, 1895.
  • H. Edelsbrunner, D. Letscher, A. Zomorodian, “Topological Persistence and Simplification,” Discrete & Computational Geometry 28, 2002, pp. 511–533 (originally FOCS 2000).
  • G. Carlsson, “Topology and Data,” Bulletin of the AMS 46(2), April 2009, pp. 255–308.
  • M. Nicolau, A. J. Levine, G. Carlsson, “Topology based data analysis identifies a subgroup of breast cancers with a unique mutational profile and excellent survival,” PNAS 108(17), 2011, pp. 7265–7270. doi:10.1073/pnas.1102826108.
  • V. de Silva, R. Ghrist, “Coverage in sensor networks via persistent homology,” Algebraic & Geometric Topology 7, 2007, pp. 339–358.

Boolean algebra → digital circuit design

  • G. Boole, An Investigation of the Laws of Thought, Walton & Maberly, 1854.
  • B. Russell, “Recent Work on the Principles of Mathematics,” The International Monthly, 1901; reprinted in Mysticism and Logic and Other Essays, 1918.
  • C. Shannon, “A Symbolic Analysis of Relay and Switching Circuits,” MIT master's thesis, 1937; abridged in Transactions of the AIEE 57(12), 1938, pp. 713–723.
  • H. H. Goldstine, The Computer from Pascal to von Neumann, Princeton University Press, 1972, pp. 119–120.

Category theory → monads in functional programming

  • S. Eilenberg, S. Mac Lane, “General Theory of Natural Equivalences,” Transactions of the AMS 58, 1945, pp. 231–294.
  • J. Rotman, review, Bulletin of the AMS 33(4), 1996, pp. 473–476 (attributes “abstract nonsense” to Steenrod); C. McLarty, British Journal for the Philosophy of Science 41, 1990, p. 355 (same attribution).
  • E. Moggi, “Computational lambda-calculus and monads,” LICS 1989; “Notions of Computation and Monads,” Information and Computation 93(1), 1991, pp. 55–92.
  • P. Wadler, “Comprehending Monads,” LFP 1990; “The Essence of Functional Programming,” POPL 1992. S. Peyton Jones, P. Wadler, “Imperative Functional Programming,” POPL 1993. Haskell 1.3, 1996 (monadic I/O standardized).

Poincaré's three-body problem → chaos theory

  • H. Poincaré, corrected memoir, Acta Mathematica 13, December 1890, pp. 1–270. Discovery history: J. Barrow-Green, Poincaré and the Three Body Problem, American Mathematical Society, 1997.
  • E. Lorenz, “Deterministic Nonperiodic Flow,” Journal of the Atmospheric Sciences 20(2), March 1963, pp. 130–141.
  • J. Gleick, Chaos: Making a New Science, Viking, 1987 (popularized the Poincaré–Smale–Lorenz connection via Smale's horseshoe map).

Hilbert spaces → quantum mechanics

  • D. Hilbert, Grundzüge einer allgemeinen Theorie der linearen Integralgleichungen, 1912 (collecting 1904–1910 papers).
  • L. A. Steen, “Highlights in the History of Spectral Theory,” American Mathematical Monthly 80(4), 1973, pp. 359–381 (source of the widely repeated “no presentiment” quote; primary provenance unconfirmed).
  • J. von Neumann, “Allgemeine Eigenwerttheorie Hermitescher Funktionaloperatoren,” Mathematische Annalen 102, 1929, pp. 49–131 (names “Hilbert space”).
  • J. von Neumann, Mathematische Grundlagen der Quantenmechanik, Julius Springer, 1932.

See something wrong?

Every case here is tagged with a confidence level for a reason — some of this is primary-source verified, some rests on reputable secondary attribution that hasn't been traced to its origin. If you can tighten a citation, correct a date, or supply the missing provenance for one of the medium-confidence quotes, open an issue or a PR on microprediction/pure.

Corrections and additional cases welcome — file an issue on microprediction/pure.
